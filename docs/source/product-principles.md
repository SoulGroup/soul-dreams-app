# The Product Principles

*What the app is beyond the reading, and which conventions of the field we refuse.*

**Product & Design**

*No matter where you are on your journey, the steps you take are equally important.*

**What kind of document this is.** A Commitments document. It is permanent. It changes
only when both founders agree we were wrong about something permanent, and the change
arrives as a dated block appended at the end, with its reasons. The text above a block
is never edited. Whether this document is signed and in force is not stated here: the
change record says so (product/product-change-record.md), never the document itself.

**What it governs, and what it defers to.** It governs what the app is beyond the
reading. It defers to The Foundations on everything a dreamer experiences of the
practice, and to Core Commitments on the company. Where the Requirements List

and restates nothing. A rule living in two documents is a defect; if you find one here,
the Requirements List governs and the line here is the defect.

**The one test, which governs every decision here.** Does this make the dreamer more the
author of their own meaning, doing real work that costs something — or more comfortable
and more dependent? Build the first. Refuse the second. (The Shared Core.)

**Rulings made in this project.** Three things here were ruled in this project and nowhere
else: §4a sending the founder's starting design for the dreamer's words to How the AI

## 1. What "state of the art" means here

It has two halves, and the second is the unusual one.

**Craft.** The mechanics are as good as the best apps in the field. Capture works
half-asleep, in the dark, and offline. Any night is found in seconds, by the dreamer's
own word (Requirements List S6). Every language the app offers reads as native (§6 below).

**Footprint.** The app is judged by whether the dreamer could do what they came for and
leave. It is never judged by how long they stayed. Time in app is measured and may be
reported; it is never a target and never a reason to change anything (Core Commitments
§3 and §3.1). Nothing here creates a new number to judge the app by; what may be counted
is the Requirements List §6, and nothing else.

## 2. Friction belongs in the work, never in the machinery

The practice keeps its friction: the mirror, the questions, the stance, the asking, the
bridge, the readings, the dreamer's own turn. That friction is the work, and it stays
(The Foundations §4.2 and §4.3).

The interface removes friction from everything else. No designed pauses. No ceremony
before a reading. No step that exists to make the app feel considered.

**One confirmation, and only one place for it.** Deletion has one confirmation (C126-I
item 6). What it covers is a night, or the whole journal; nowhere else does the app ask
"are you sure". Product's own ruling, recorded at C4-P.

Two things come before anything is recorded and are not ceremony: the age question and
the tester disclosure. Both are ruled requirements (Requirements List X2; C122-I item 1;
C123-I item 4). The design makes each as short as its ruling allows, and no shorter.

## 3. Accessibility

**The floor.** The current version of WCAG, at level AA, everywhere in the app. The
disclosure, the journal, the readings, the settings — nothing is exempt.

**The defining case.** A person at six in the morning, in the dark, perhaps without
their glasses, who wants to say a few lines and go back to sleep. Every capture design is
tested against that person first. This case is reachable because capture is capture
only and asks nothing; the mirror is the first step of being read, and comes only when
the dreamer chooses to be read (C124-I; Requirements List O1 and O2).

**Assistive technology is told what changed.** When new content appears — a reading, a
failure sentence, a saved state — a screen reader is told so in plain words. WCAG
requires this at level AA: Success Criterion 4.1.3, Status Messages `[READ, W3C,
"Understanding SC 4.1.3", 2026-10-08]`. The announcement states what happened and nothing
more. It never celebrates (§4b). Its words are founder copy.

**The microphone.** Permission for the microphone is asked when voice is first used, and
not before (C126-I item 3).

## 4. The conventions of the field we refuse

Most of what the field calls best practice is built to keep a person in an app. This app
limits its own footprint in a person's life (the Shared Core; Core Commitments §4). So
these are refused, each with its reason. The list is permanent. Anything a design wants
to add from the field and is not here is new, and is brought to the founders as new.

### 4a. The chat thread

The app is not a person the dreamer talks to. The app's words appear as information, not
as a person speaking. The dreamer's words are always visibly theirs. The app has no name,
no avatar and no character.

The reason: a night has four voices — the dreamer, the app, the app through a named
tradition, and a quoted source — and each must be recognisable (Requirements List C1;
C122-I item 6). A chat thread makes the app one more speaker, and a speaker invites the
reply a conversation invites. The five lines the practice already draws for how these
voices are kept apart are C122-I item 6, and above all its last: no voice or tradition is
styled as more important than another.

How a tradition's voice and a quoted source look, and the founder's starting design for
the dreamer's words, are decided in How the AI Appears, written with Interpretation. They
are not decided here. Product's own ruling, recorded at C4-P.

### 4b. The variable reward

The readings differ every night. That is the shape of a hook, and this app does nothing
to make it one.

Nothing amplifies the reading or signals success. No reveal animation. No sound or
haptic. No completion fill. No checkmark. No colour that means good. No line announcing
that a reading is ready. The reading appears; nothing performs it.

Whatever shows that the app is working shows work, never progress toward a reward, and
never ends in a flourish. How it looks is decided in How the AI Appears. A reading that
fails is never shown in part (C122-I item 5).

The reason: anything that rewards the moment a reading arrives teaches a person to come
back for the arrival, not for the work.

### 4c. Onboarding as activation

Onboarding teaches how to use the app and sets the person up to start as soon as
possible. That is all it does.

It asks the age once and keeps only the gate's result (C122-I item 1; C123-I item 4). It
asks nothing about who the person is. No name, no email, no sign-up and no account (C2-P
item 6). An account, if one is ever offered, is optional and created from within the app
(C123-I item 3), and keeps every rule here.

No permission is asked before it is needed, and none is ever asked to notify (§4i). The
microphone is asked for when voice is first used (§3).

A first night that is capture only is a whole first use (Requirements List O2 and C10).
Nothing in onboarding steers toward being read.

The reason: onboarding that activates is built to produce a first reading. This app is
built to let a person record where they are.

### 4d. Implicit memory

The app keeps nothing you did not set or give yourself. An open session comes back to
where you left it.

What a dreamer sets or gives: the language, the age gate's result, the consent in the
disclosure. What the app never keeps: how they use it, what they look at, when they
come, what they did last time. Memory of dreams across nights — what returns — is the
product; memory of the dreamer is refused (the Shared Core; The Foundations §4.4; X7).

The reason: an interface that remembers is an interface that adapts, and an app that
adapts to a dreamer has started to learn them.

### 4e. Testing on dreamers

Nothing a dreamer meets is A/B tested. No event analytics. No tracking of what a dreamer
does. The Requirements List X12 already refuses analytics that collect wholesale; this
goes further and refuses them altogether.

What Product learns from: the founders' own design research; the prototype clicked
through by three people who are not beta testers, on invented dreams only (C2-P item 3);
and what testers choose to tell us. Giving feedback is made very easy in the beta, under
the rules at C126-I item 4 (§5). Never from tracking anyone.

Two things are not analytics and stand. The records the practice counts only across all
dreamers leave the device as totals, carrying nothing that identifies anyone (C123-I
item 2). Crash and error reports may leave the device, with no night content and no
install ID, configured so they cannot carry the dreamer's words (C126-I item 2).

The reason: an app tested on its users is tuned to what keeps them. That is the thing we
refuse to optimise for.

### 4f. Growth surfaces

None, anywhere in the app. No review prompts. No sharing — nothing in the app is
shareable (C122-I, its NOTED line). No referral (Core Commitments §4.1). Nothing is ever
sold inside the practice: not in a session, not on a night. What may be sold, and where,
is decided when there is something to sell, and not here. Product's own ruling, recorded
at C4-P.

The reason: a growth surface asks the dreamer to do something for the company. Inside
this app the dreamer does things only for themselves.

### 4g. Export

Whether the app offers export is not a permanent matter; it depends on what the law and
counsel say, and the change record holds where it stands (C123-I item 1; C122-I item 11).
What is permanent: if export comes, it keeps §4h. Leaving with one's own words costs no
more than arriving, and no export is ever a growth surface.

### 4h. Lock-in

Leaving costs no more than arriving. Deleting a night, or the whole journal, is done from
one obvious place, with the one confirmation §2 allows, and no retention flow: nothing
asks the dreamer to stay, nothing offers an alternative, nothing delays. Deleting the
journal, or removing the app, removes every night (C122-I item 2; Requirements List X11).
Any export or account that comes later keeps this.

What that sentence does not cover is said the way C126-I item 1 says it, everywhere: no
sign-up, no account; a night's words leave the device only to be read; the totals that
leave carry nothing that identifies anyone (C123-I item 2). Where a night is read, and what
is kept there, is the tester disclosure's to state (C126-I item 1).

The reason: a practice that limits its own footprint cannot make itself hard to leave.

### 4i. Reaching out

The app never reaches the dreamer. It never notifies, never asks permission to notify,
never puts a count on its icon, never puts anything on the lock screen except the capture
shortcut below, never sends email. The
Requirements List X7 and Core Commitments §4.1 allow one thing an app may do here; this
app does not use it (C126-I item 3).

One thing is allowed: a shortcut to capture — on a home screen, a widget, a lock screen —
if, and only if, it opens capture and shows nothing. No count, no last night, no words
from the journal.

The reason: every message from an app is a claim on a person's attention made on the
app's initiative. This app acts only on the dreamer's.

## 5. The surfaces Product designs beyond the reading

Most of these surfaces have no requirement of their own; where one exists, it is pointed
at. Product designs them all. Each comes to Interpretation with its design, in the
Experience Map, before anything is built (C126-I item 6; the Requirements List §9 items 2,
3 and 4). The one test, applied to each:

- **The age question.** One question, kept only as its result (C122-I item 1; C123-I
  item 4). The test: it keeps the app honest about who it is for, and it learns nothing.
- **The tester disclosure's screen.** One document, one consent (Requirements List X2;
  its words are W27). The test: a person who knows what the app will not do is the author
  of what they bring to it. The design gives the words room to be read, and never hurries
  the person past them.
- **How to use the app.** It teaches use. It never explains the practice in a way the
  Requirements List does not name (§9 item 2). The test: it makes the dreamer able, and
  sets nothing up for them.
- **Settings.** Only what the dreamer sets themselves (§4d). Language is the one setting.
  The test: no setting changes how the practice works, only which language it is read and
  shown in.
- **Language choice.** The dreamer chooses from the languages the app offers. Changing it
  changes the app's fixed words only; a night stays in the language it was read in (C122-I
  item 4). The test: the dreamer owns the language of their own record.
- **Deletion.** One obvious place, one confirmation (C126-I item 6), nothing else (§2,
  §4h). The test: leaving is the dreamer's and costs nothing.
- **Empty states.** The journal with no nights, a search with no result (W25), a night with
  nothing beside what was written (W22). The test: an empty state says what is there, and
  never that something should be.
- **Offline capture.** Capture works with no connection. A voice recording waits on the
  device until it can be transcribed, and is then deleted (C126-I item 5; X9). The dreamer
  is told what will happen to what they recorded, and when (W29). The test: the practice
  does not depend on the network at six in the morning.
- **Failure states.** A reading that fails is never shown in part: one plain sentence,
  and the night stays as captured, to be read again (C122-I item 5). The test: a failure
  is the app's and is said so; it is never the dreamer's.
- **What is kept, and what leaves the device, shown plainly.** The dreamer can see what
  the app keeps and what leaves. It says what C126-I item 1 says: no sign-up, no account; a
  night's words leave the device only to be read; the totals that leave carry nothing that
  identifies anyone. Voice is transcribed and then gone (X9). The test: a dreamer who can
  see what is kept is the author of what they give.
- **The crisis-resources page.** One static page, reachable from every screen, never
  offered; kept as filed for Cohort 1 (C126-I item 6; Requirements List X1). The test: a
  person who needs it can reach it, and no one is steered to it.
- **Feedback, in the beta.** Giving feedback is very easy. Its rules are C126-I item 4: it
  asks about the app and never invites a dream; what is written goes to the founders only.
  It is the only way the app hears from a tester, and it is never tracking (§4e). The test:
  a tester who speaks is an author; a tester who is watched is a subject.

Nothing here is a screen. Screens are drawn by the founder, in Figma, after this and the
Experience Map.

## 6. Languages

The app starts in English and Swedish (Requirements List X13). Every language it ever
offers keeps one rule: every dreamer-facing line is written by a person in that language,
independently — never machine-translated, never drafted by a model or a chat. A night is
read once, in one language, and kept in it (C122-I item 4). Adding a language goes to
Interpretation first: the practice must be readable in it, not only the screens.

Product designs the places where words go, and how long a line may be in each language.
The founders write the lines. A place that has no words yet is marked as one:
[founder copy: what the line must do]. Every place these surfaces need is held in the
Places Register, not here.
