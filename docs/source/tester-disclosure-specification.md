# Tester Disclosure — Specification

**2026-09-30 · Interpretation · Head of Interpretation.** Specifies what the disclosure must
do. The words are the founders', written in each language independently, never
machine-translated, never model-drafted. This document is not the disclosure. It replaces the
specification of 2026-09-15 and folds in that document's appended block of the same date; both
are in its archive note verbatim (C83-I item 9).

*No matter where you are on your journey, the steps you take are equally important.*

**Why it exists.** The distress apparatus is deferred, not ruled out, and no cohort is held back
waiting for it (The Foundations, "Where we stop", as its dated block's Item 6 writes it; C73-I
item 2). Detection runs and consequence is deferred (C51-I). Core Commitments has committed the
company to giving every tester a written disclosure before they record anything. This
specification is Interpretation's, as owner of the law document the disclosure stands in for.

---

## 1. What is true, and must be said plainly

**The app classifies and records but does not respond.** Every capture and every dreamer turn
is classified, and every firing is recorded. Nothing acts on it. This is the single fact the
disclosure exists to convey, and it is the fact most likely to be softened in drafting.

⚠️ **"The app does not detect distress" is FALSE and must not be written.** It detects and
does nothing. A tester told nothing is watching, who later learns their nights were classified
and stored, has been misled by us.

## 2. What the disclosure must do — six things

1. **State that the app will not respond.** It will not notice out loud, will not stop,
   will not change what it asks, and will continue in its ordinary way however the night goes.
2. **State that the app does classify and record, and say what is stored, who can read it, and
   for how long.** What it must say about each, because each is ruled:
   - **Stored with the night.** Every firing is stored with the night it occurred in, as part of
     that night — a firing at capture has a night and may have no session (C63-I item 3).
     Nothing indexes firings by tester.
   - **Read only as one total.** In Cohort 1 the firing rate is read only as one total across
     the cohort, never sliced by time and never by tester (C63-I item 5).
   - **Who reads, and how.** Firings are reviewed during beta to tune the signals. A reviewer
     reads one night's firing at a time and never assembles one tester's nights. At four testers
     anonymity is impossible, and the disclosure does not claim it: the protection is the rule,
     the consent and this disclosure (C63-I item 4).
   - **The other per-night records.** Each night also records whether a tradition read because
     of the bridge answer, stored with the night and counted only across all dreamers (C80-I
     item 1; C83-I item 8). The disclosure says so.
   - **For how long.** No ruling states it. The founders' text must say it, and says nothing it
     cannot keep.
3. **Obtain consent for that storage**, in the same text. A classification stored with
   nothing acting on it is agreed to in advance or it does not happen. This is Core
   Commitments' precondition and it lives here. Classifications are stored from Cohort 0 onward
   with nothing acting on them, which is why this consent is required and not optional (C51-I).
4. **State that crisis resources are reachable from the app at all times, and that nothing
   will offer them.** The tester fetches them. Name where.
5. **State that stopping is theirs, at any point, and costs nothing** — and who reads their
   dreams.
6. **State what the app is not for.** Plainly, at the point a tester is told what the app will
   not do: this is not therapy, and there are things it is not meant to hold. Not a warning and
   not a restriction on what may be written — a dreamer who wants to bring their most intimate
   fears and thoughts is allowed to, and the app never tells anyone what to do or how to act.
   What the sentence establishes is what the app will and will not be, so nobody arrives
   expecting the other thing. It sits beside the statement that the app classifies and records
   but does not respond.

## 3. What it must not do

- **Reassure.** The disclosure replaces detection. One that reads as an apology for missing
  detection recreates the expectation it exists to remove.
- **Soften the absence** or imply it is temporary.
- **Promise a future** in which the app does notice.
- **Describe what a tester should do if distressed**, beyond naming where the resources are.
  That is the app pressing before a session has begun.
- **Claim anonymity** (§2 item 2).

## 4. Form

One document. Read before anything is recorded. Consent given in it, not separately — the beta
access consent included: one document, one consent (C64-I item 4). Written by the founders in
Swedish and English independently. Cohort 1's first text and on the critical path.

**It adds no third text.** The count of founder-written texts before the first non-founder
dreamer is two: this disclosure and the always-reachable crisis resources surface. For later
cohorts the substance of §2 item 6 becomes onboarding copy, founder-written, batched to Product &
Design's Copy Register.
